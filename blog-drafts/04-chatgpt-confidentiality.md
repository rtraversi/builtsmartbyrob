# Can Your Firm Use ChatGPT Without Breaking Confidentiality?

This is the question I get more than any other, and the honest answer is: *it depends entirely on which version you're using and how it's configured.* The gap between "fine" and "serious problem" is enormous, and it usually comes down to settings most people never look at.

Here's how I think about it when I set these tools up for a firm.

## The real risk isn't AI — it's where your data goes

When you paste text into a free, consumer AI tool, you're sending client information to a third party. Depending on the tool and its settings, that text may be used to train future models, stored on servers you don't control, or reviewed by humans for quality. For most businesses that's an annoyance. For a law firm, it can implicate your duty of confidentiality.

That's the whole game. The AI isn't the problem. *Uncontrolled data leaving your office* is the problem.

## The three tiers, in plain terms

**1. Free consumer tools (highest risk).** The free tier of a public chatbot is the one to be careful with. Assume anything you type could be retained or used to improve the product unless you've explicitly turned that off. This is not where client matters belong.

**2. Business / "Team" / "Enterprise" tiers (much better).** Paid business versions of the major AI tools generally offer a critical promise: your inputs are *not* used to train their models, and data handling is governed by a business agreement. This is the minimum bar I'd want before any real client information goes near a tool.

**3. Private or self-contained setups (most control).** For sensitive work, the data can be kept inside an environment you control, so nothing client-related is exposed to a public service at all. This is the model I prefer to build for firms that handle anything truly sensitive.

## What I'd actually check before trusting a tool

- **Training opt-out:** Is there a clear, *on-by-default-for-business* guarantee that your inputs won't train their models?
- **A real agreement:** Is there a Data Processing Agreement or business terms — not just a consumer privacy policy?
- **Retention:** How long is your data kept, and can you turn off history?
- **Access:** Who at the vendor can see what you submit?
- **Your own policy:** Does your team actually know which tool to use for what? The best setup fails if someone pastes a client file into the wrong window.

## The practical takeaway

You don't have to ban AI to protect confidentiality, and you don't have to trust it blindly either. You have to be deliberate about *which* tool and *which* settings — and then make sure your whole team uses the same one the same way.

That last part — turning "we should be careful" into an actual configured, documented setup — is most of what I do for firms. If you're not sure which tier you're currently on, that's the first thing worth finding out.
